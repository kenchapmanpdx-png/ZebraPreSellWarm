## Context

We have 35 prerendered ingredient pages live at `https://www.wellnessforzebras.com/ingredients/<slug>`. Each page already renders the full clinical data from `client/src/data/ingredients.ts` — about 870 words per page of research-voice content covering: at-a-glance, mechanism of action, research studies with PMIDs, MCAS/hEDS/POTS-specific guidance (triad block), dose protocol, form rationale, safety, sources.

What's missing is **patient-language framing**. Current pages use clinical voice ("PEA achieved 54.3% inhibition of histamine release via CB2/DAGL pathway"). Real patients search in their own words ("natural antihistamine for mast cell", "best supplement for histamine intolerance"). Adding a plain-language layer at the TOP of each page captures both audiences without diluting the protocol.

The pages serve a real audience: people with hypermobile EDS, POTS, MCAS, often complex polypharmacy, often extremely sensitive to supplements and excipients. Honesty about evidence gaps matters. False medical claims create FTC and personal harm risk.

## Compliance guardrails (non-negotiable)

- **No diagnose / treat / cure / prevent language.** "Helps with," "supports," "may reduce" are OK. "Treats POTS" is not.
- **No drug-comparison superiority claims.** "More effective than X" or "replaces X" is off-limits.
- **Honesty about evidence quality.** If a finding is from in vitro or animal models, say so. If hEDS-specific data is absent, say so. The research protocol explicitly requires this.
- **Respect the TGF-β paradox.** Most mast cell stabilizers can suppress collagen synthesis at high doses. If the protocol flags this for an ingredient, the patient content should reflect the trade-off, not gloss over it.
- **MMP framing.** hEDS involves excess collagen DEGRADATION (53-fold MMP-1 upregulation), not synthesis deficiency. "Boosts collagen" is wrong framing. "Protects existing collagen" or "supports cross-linking" is right.

## What I'm asking the chat to produce

For each of the 35 ingredients in `CONTENT_HANDOFF_INGREDIENTS.json` (companion file), write three short pieces of plain-language content:

### Piece 1: Patient summary (60–110 words)

Opens the page. Goes before any clinical content. Answers a patient's three questions:

1. What is this?
2. Why might it matter for me (EDS / POTS / MCAS framing)?
3. What does it do in plain language?

Tone: knowledgeable friend, not lecturer. Reading level ~10th grade. No jargon without immediate translation. Match search-query phrasing where possible — patients type "mast cell stabilizer" and "histamine release" more than "MCAS pathophysiology."

### Piece 2: 4 FAQ items (per ingredient)

Real questions patients would type into Google. Each answer 40–80 words. Pull from the existing clinical data in the project — you have access to the research; the chat does not need to invent. If the data is silent, write the answer as "What we know / What we don't know" with explicit acknowledgment of gaps.

Suggested question slots (adapt per ingredient):
- "What does [ingredient] actually do?"
- "How is this different from the cheap version at [common retailer]?"
- "Is [ingredient] safe with [common POTS/MCAS medications]?"
- "How long until I notice anything?"
- "Will this make my MCAS / POTS / EDS worse?"

Pick the 4 most relevant to that ingredient's mechanism and patient concerns. Some ingredients have strong drug-interaction stories (warfarin + K2). Some have a counterfeit / quality issue (NR, NMN). Some have a TGF-β paradox concern. The questions should reflect the ingredient's actual issues, not be boilerplate.

### Piece 3: Plain-language triad rewrite (3 paragraphs)

The existing `triad.mcas`, `triad.heds`, `triad.pots` fields are in clinical voice. Rewrite each in patient voice, same length budget (~80–120 words each). Keep all the substantive claims and mechanism references; change only the vocabulary and reading level. If a claim is too speculative to support in patient voice, drop it rather than soften it.

## What I'm NOT asking for

- **Don't rewrite the existing clinical content.** It stays as-is, after the plain-language additions.
- **Don't invent research findings.** Use what's in the protocol. If something is unknown, say it's unknown.
- **Don't write meta descriptions / titles.** Those are auto-generated by the prerender script from the data.
- **Don't add FTC disclaimers in body content.** Those go in the global footer separately.
- **Don't suggest specific brands or dose changes.** Doses are set by the protocol; brands are set by the formulation team.
